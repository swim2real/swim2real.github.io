Swim2Real: Vision-Language Models for Simulator Calibration of a Soft Robotic Fish

Under Review

TL;DR: Swim2Real turns VLM physical diagnoses into objective-checked simulator updates. In a preregistered fifty-seed replication and with a second VLM, the proposed directions reduce calibration error relative to random and signed-permuted directions under the same line search.

Abstract

Calibrating a soft aquatic robot simulator requires matching coupled deformation and propulsion from limited pool recordings. Swim2Real uses a vision-language model (VLM) to compare simulated and real swimming motion and propose updates to 16 fluid, motor, and joint parameters, and a backtracking line search accepts an update only when it reduces marker error. To test whether the proposals carry useful directions, we compare them with random and signed-permuted directions under the same line search and simulator budget. In an independent, preregistered fifty-seed replication, Swim2Real reduces the area under the best-so-far error curve over 40 calls (AUC40) by a paired mean of 63.4 mm against random directions and 53.2 mm against permuted directions (both Holm p < 10⁻⁵, conditional on run completion). A separate 24-seed experiment with GLM-5.3-Flash also favors the method against both controls, and an earlier fifteen-seed study favored it in magnitude without passing Holm correction. Backtracking uses 2.4 times fewer VLM proposals than Vid2Sid, which attains lower error at the same simulator budget. Ten original hardware trials demonstrate open-loop execution of simulator-generated motor commands.

Method Detail

VLM Interface and Objective Gate

Swim2Real treats the VLM output as a candidate direction, not as an accepted calibration. Each VLM round receives side-by-side simulated and real marker overlays, the real video at the worst-matching frequency, per-marker and per-frequency errors, velocities, the current 16-parameter vector, fixed bounds, and recent proposal outcomes.

The response is parsed into a physical diagnosis, parameter names, proposed values, and a confidence estimate. The parser accepts only known parameter names, clamps proposed values to fixed bounds, and leaves unmentioned parameters unchanged. Backtracking then checks the full proposal and two smaller steps under the same 40-evaluation simulation budget. Only objective-improving candidates are accepted, so the VLM supplies physical hypotheses while the marker objective selects the calibration step.

Evaluation

Results

A fifteen-seed matched-control study compares the complete method against random and signed-permuted directions under the identical line search, together with Bayesian optimization, SPSA, two budget-matched CMA-ES variants, and Vid2Sid, which requests a fresh VLM proposal for every candidate. Among the seven arms other than Vid2Sid, Swim2Real has the lowest mean AUC40 (93.5 ± 40.8 mm), completes every simulator call, and reaches 55 mm in a median of 16 evaluations against 33 to 37 for the matched controls. Both preregistered paired contrasts favor it in estimated magnitude, but neither passes the preregistered Holm rule, so this study keeps its non-confirmatory classification and is reported descriptively. Vid2Sid attains lower mean error at the same simulator budget with 39 proposals per run against 16.1.

An independent replication ran the complete method and both matched controls on fifty fresh paired seeds under the same frozen protocol, with contrasts, test, and reporting rules fixed before launch. Over 42 primary pairs, the paired mean AUC40 differences are 63.4 mm over random directions and 53.2 mm over signed-permuted VLM directions, with rank-biserial correlations of -0.87 and -0.75. Both contrasts pass the preregistered Holm rule at p < 10⁻⁵, so the replication confirms on independent seeds the effect the fifteen-seed campaign observed descriptively. The two studies are reported side by side and never pooled.

A secondary open-weight panel repeats the protocol with GLM-5.3-Flash under the same budget, line search, and analysis, and beats both matched controls: median AUC40 is 71.1 mm against 161.1 mm for random directions and 113.2 mm for signed-permuted directions, with Holm p < 10⁻⁴. The confirmatory evidence for the main claims remains one-backbone, and we make no backbone-independence claim.

An eight-seed interpolation study fits five nominal frequencies and assesses the frozen parameters at three withheld frequencies. Swim2Real reaches 53.1 ± 3.8 mm mean assessment error, against 102.2 ± 64.9 mm for random directions and 65.4 ± 28.5 mm for Bayesian optimization, and wins 7 of 8 paired initializations against random directions and 6 of 8 against BO. The recordings were seen during development, so this is an interpolation check rather than fresh-data generalization.

Hardware Scope

Open-Loop Hardware Replay

The hardware check executes stored motor commands open loop, with the physical fish and the MuJoCo simulator replaying the same recorded command sequence. Across ten open-loop trials, mean signed displacement along the initial body heading was +0.382 ± 0.220 m and was positive in 10 of 10 trials under a rotation-test-validated body frame; mean net displacement was 0.566 ± 0.344 m, with forward and lateral components of comparable magnitude. Because these commands were replayed open loop and the calibration objective removes global position and heading, the trials establish command-execution feasibility rather than closed-loop transfer.

Code, data, the frozen evaluator, and the verbatim VLM prompt template will be released upon publication. The exact prompt template and prompt-builder source are available for anonymous review at anonymous.4open.science.
