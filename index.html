Swim2Real: Vision-Language Models for Simulator Calibration of a Soft Robotic Fish

TL;DR: Swim2Real turns VLM physical diagnoses into objective-checked simulator updates. In a preregistered fifty-seed replication and with a second VLM, the proposed directions reduce calibration error relative to random and signed-permuted directions under the same line search and simulator budget.

Abstract

Calibrating a soft aquatic robot simulator requires matching coupled deformation and propulsion from limited pool recordings. Swim2Real uses a vision-language model (VLM) to compare real and simulated swimming videos and propose updates to 16 simulator parameters. A backtracking line search tests each direction and accepts updates that reduce marker error. To test whether the proposals carry useful directions, we compare them with random and signed-permuted directions under the same line search and simulator budget. In an independent, preregistered fifty-seed replication, Swim2Real reduces the mean best-so-far error over 40 simulator evaluations by 63.4 mm against random directions and 53.2 mm against permuted directions. Both paired comparisons pass Holm correction (p < 10⁻⁵), conditional on run completion. A 24-seed experiment with a second VLM supports the same direction effect. Backtracking averages 16.1 VLM proposals per run versus 39 for Vid2Sid, which attains lower error at the same simulator budget. Ten hardware trials demonstrate open-loop execution of simulator-generated motor commands.

Method Detail

VLM Interface and Objective Gate

Swim2Real treats the VLM output as a candidate direction, not as an accepted calibration. Each VLM round receives side-by-side simulated and real marker overlays, the real video at the worst-matching frequency, per-marker and per-frequency errors, velocities, the current 16-parameter vector, fixed bounds, and recent proposal outcomes.

The response is parsed into a physical diagnosis, parameter names, proposed values, and a confidence estimate. The parser accepts only known parameter names, clamps proposed values to fixed bounds, and leaves unmentioned parameters unchanged. Backtracking then checks the full proposal and two smaller steps under the same 40-evaluation simulation budget. Only objective-improving candidates are accepted, so the VLM supplies physical hypotheses while the marker objective selects the calibration step.

Evaluation

Results

A fifteen-seed matched-control study compares the complete method against random and signed-permuted directions under the identical line search, together with Bayesian optimization, SPSA, two budget-matched CMA-ES variants, and Vid2Sid, which requests a fresh VLM proposal for every candidate. Among the seven arms other than Vid2Sid, Swim2Real has the lowest mean AUC40 (93.5 ± 40.8 mm), completes every simulator call, and reaches 55 mm in a median of 16 evaluations against 33 to 37 for the matched controls. Both preregistered paired contrasts favor it in estimated magnitude, but neither passes the preregistered Holm rule, so this study keeps its non-confirmatory classification and is reported descriptively. Vid2Sid attains lower mean error at the same simulator budget with 39 proposals per run against 16.1.

An independent replication ran the complete method and both matched controls on fifty fresh paired seeds. The paired analysis includes 41 initializations completed by all three methods and one shared initialization that failed identically in all three methods. That common failure receives a 1000 mm penalty in each method and contributes a zero paired difference. Over these 42 analyzed initializations, the paired mean AUC40 differences are 63.4 mm over random directions and 53.2 mm over signed-permuted VLM directions, with rank-biserial correlations of -0.87 and -0.75. Both contrasts pass Holm correction at p < 10⁻⁵. Apart from the shared failure, the analysis conditions on joint completion. The earlier fifteen-seed study remains non-confirmatory, and the two studies are reported separately.

A second-model experiment evaluates 24 paired initializations with GLM-5.3-Flash, using 16 sampled frames per clip and the same simulator budget and line search. The complete and permuted arms use GLM, and the random-direction runs are reused from the replication because that control queries no model. Median AUC40 is 71.1 mm against 161.1 mm for random directions and 113.2 mm for signed-permuted directions, with Holm p < 10⁻⁴. This second-model result supports the same direction effect observed with Gemini.

An eight-seed interpolation study fits five nominal frequencies and assesses the frozen parameters at three withheld frequencies. Swim2Real reaches 53.1 ± 3.8 mm mean assessment error, against 102.2 ± 64.9 mm for random directions and 65.4 ± 28.5 mm for Bayesian optimization, and wins 7 of 8 paired initializations against random directions and 6 of 8 against BO. The recordings were seen during development, so this is an interpolation check rather than fresh-data generalization.

Hardware Scope

Open-Loop Hardware Replay

The hardware check executes stored motor commands open loop, with the physical fish and the MuJoCo simulator replaying the same recorded command sequence. The policies were trained with an earlier simulator, separately from the calibration studies reported here. Across ten open-loop trials, mean signed displacement along the initial body heading was +0.382 ± 0.220 m and was positive in 10 of 10 trials; mean net displacement was 0.566 ± 0.344 m. These trials establish the feasibility of executing simulator-generated commands on the robot. Evaluating the calibration reported here on hardware requires feedback, measured target attainment, and a matched control.
